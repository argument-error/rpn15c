RPN-15C at a glance

A functional 15C-style emulator. Memory is kept on this device between visits.

Tap the RPN plate for the side panel: the stack, registers, matrices and your program listing, with import, export, a program library and backup. Tap the 15C plate for this help.

Put it on your home screen

In Chrome on Android, open the ⋮ menu and choose Add to Home screen (or Install app). Hold the phone sideways; when it is upright the faceplate turns so you can still use it.

Basics

  • RPN: 2 ENTER 3 + gives 5. The stack is X, Y, Z, T.
  • f selects the gold function above a key, g the blue one on its front.
  • f 7 4 sets FIX 4; f 8 and 9 set SCI and ENG.
  • STO/RCL take 0–9, .0–.9, I (TAN) and (i) (COS); STO + 1 does register arithmetic.
  • Statistics: enter y ENTER x Σ+, then g x̄, s, f L.R. and ŷ,r. Data lives in R2–R7.

Programs

  • g P/R enters program mode. Example: f LBL A g x² 2 − g RTN, then g P/R again.
  • Run with f A or GSB A; any key stops a running program.
  • Edit with SST, g BST, ← (delete line) and GTO CHS nnn (go to line).
  • Supported: LBL, GTO, GSB/RTN (7 levels), R/S, PSE, DSE, ISG, x≤y, x=0, TEST 0–9, SF/CF/F?, indirect I and (i).

SOLVE and ∫

  • Put two guesses in Y and X, then f SOLVE A. You get the root in X, the previous estimate in Y and f(root) in Z; Error 8 means no root was found.
  • Put the limits in Y and X, then f ∫ A. You get the integral in X and its uncertainty in Y. The display setting controls accuracy (FIX 4 is good to about 0.00005).

Complex numbers

  • 3 ENTER 4 f I makes 3 + 4i and lights C. The display shows the real part; hold f (i) to see the imaginary part.
  • f Re≷Im swaps the parts. CLx, ←, CHS and new digits change only the real part.
  • Arithmetic, √x, x², 1/x, yˣ, eˣ, LN, 10ˣ, LOG, ABS, trig, hyperbolics and their inverses, →P and →R all work on complex numbers. Trig uses radians in complex mode.
  • Also g SF 8 turns complex mode on; g CF 8 turns it off and clears the imaginary parts.

Matrices A–E

  • Size: rows ENTER columns f DIM A (up to 64 elements each).
  • Fill in order: f MATRIX 1, turn on f USER, then value STO A repeatedly (RCL A reads back). R0 and R1 hold the row and column.
  • One element: value ENTER row ENTER column STO g A; row ENTER column RCL g A.
  • RCL MATRIX A puts the matrix itself in X (shown as A 2 2). f RESULT B picks where answers go.
  • With matrices in Y and X: + − ×, and ÷ solves a system (Y ÷ X = X⁻¹Y). 1/x inverts, CHS negates, a number in the other register works element by element.
  • f MATRIX: 0 clears all, 1 resets R0/R1, 4 transpose, 5 YᵀX, 6 residual, 7 row norm, 8 Frobenius norm, 9 determinant. 2, 3, f Py,x and g Cy,x convert complex-matrix forms.
  • Number STO MATRIX A fills every element; with a matrix in X it copies that matrix into A.

Memory and errors

  • Any key clears an Error message. Error 0 means improper math, 1 improper matrix use, 2 bad statistics, 3 bad register or element, 4 missing label or line, 5 subroutines too deep, 7 SOLVE or ∫ inside itself, 8 no root, 10 matrix too large, 11 matrix sizes don't fit.
  • ON then −: full reset (Pr Error). ON then .: swap decimal point and comma. ON then ×: display test.
  • STO f RAN # sets the random seed; RCL f RAN # reads it.

Computer keyboard

Digits, . + − * /, Enter, Backspace; f g; s STO, r RCL, x x≷y, d R↓, c CHS, e EEX, q √x.

Differences from the original

Registers are fixed at R0–R.9 and I, with 448 program lines and matrix storage kept separately, so DIM (i) isn't needed. Matrix ÷ and the determinant leave the original matrix unchanged rather than overwriting it with its LU form. Results are rounded to 10 digits after every step.
